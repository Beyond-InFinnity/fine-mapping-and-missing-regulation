# Most schizophrenia risk loci that lack brain eQTL colocalization share causal variants with DNA methylation

Connor Finnerty^1^

^1^ Independent researcher.

## Abstract

Genome-wide association studies of schizophrenia have identified hundreds of risk loci, most of which are presumed to act through gene regulation. Colocalization analyses nevertheless assign a candidate effector gene to only a minority of loci, a discrepancy that has been termed the missing regulation problem. Here we quantify this discrepancy and evaluate competing explanations for it using exclusively public summary statistics. Of 281 analysable loci from the PGC3 schizophrenia meta-analysis, 90 (32%) colocalized (PP4 > 0.8) with an expression QTL in at least one of 14 bulk brain datasets, although 94% of the remaining loci harbored a strong local eQTL. Colocalization scaled with eQTL sample size (odds ratio 2.5 per tenfold increase, p = 3.8 x 10^-6^) but approached a ceiling near 25%. Fine-mapping with SuSiE showed that 40% of loci contain multiple independent signals; pairing signals individually reclassified 27% of single-variant colocalization calls in each direction but did not preferentially explain the failures. Single-cell, fetal, and splicing QTLs together accounted for a further 19 loci. In contrast, brain DNA methylation QTLs colocalized at 193 of 281 loci, including 118 of the 191 loci without expression colocalization, at a per-test rate six times that of expression. Sequential attribution across all six channels left 65 loci (23%) unexplained. A transitive analysis linking risk variants to genes through shared methylation signals nominated candidate genes at 44 loci that lack direct eQTL support. These results indicate that most schizophrenia risk variants without eQTL colocalization are nonetheless active on brain chromatin, and that current expression catalogs, rather than the regulatory hypothesis itself, account for much of the missing regulation.

## Introduction

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of schizophrenia have been notably successful at identifying risk loci: the most recent Psychiatric Genomics Consortium meta-analysis (PGC3) reported 287 genome-wide significant regions from 67,390 cases and 94,015 controls (Trubetskoy et al., 2022). As in other complex traits, the great majority of associated variants are non-coding, and the prevailing model holds that they act by altering the regulation of nearby genes. Under this model, expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) measured in disease-relevant tissue should identify the target genes, and statistical colocalization (Giambartolomei et al., 2014) provides a principled test of whether a GWAS association and an eQTL share a single causal variant.

In practice, colocalization between GWAS loci and eQTLs is observed far less often than the regulatory model predicts. Estimates across complex traits suggest that only 20-40% of GWAS loci colocalize with an eQTL in any assayed tissue, and the proportion for brain disorders is typically lower. Connally et al. (2022) framed this discrepancy as the missing regulation problem and argued that GWAS loci and eQTLs are systematically mismatched with respect to the genes and regulatory elements they implicate. Several non-exclusive explanations have been proposed: insufficient eQTL sample sizes; violation of the single-causal-variant assumption of standard colocalization methods at loci with allelic heterogeneity; dilution of cell-type-specific effects in bulk tissue; regulatory effects restricted to developmental windows not represented in adult post-mortem collections; and effects on molecular processes other than steady-state transcript abundance.

These explanations have generally been examined in isolation, on different traits, and with heterogeneous methods, which makes their relative contributions difficult to judge. Schizophrenia is a suitable test case: the GWAS is well powered, brain eQTL resources span an order of magnitude in sample size, and public summary statistics are available for single-cell, fetal, splicing, and DNA methylation QTLs in brain tissue.

Here we evaluate these explanations within a single reproducible framework applied to the PGC3 schizophrenia loci, using only publicly available summary statistics. We first replicate the missing regulation observation across 14 bulk brain expression datasets. We then quantify, in turn, the contributions of allelic heterogeneity, eQTL power, cell-type resolution, developmental context, splicing, and DNA methylation, and we attribute each locus to the first molecular channel that explains it. Finally, we use shared methylation signals to nominate candidate genes at loci that lack direct eQTL support, and we characterize the residual set of loci that no tested channel explains.

## Results

### A minority of schizophrenia loci colocalize with brain eQTLs

Of the 287 PGC3 loci, we excluded the extended MHC region and five loci on chromosome X, leaving 281 for analysis. For each locus we tested colocalization between the GWAS signal and cis-eQTLs for every gene within 1 Mb of the index variant, in 13 GTEx v8 brain tissues (n = 114-209), and in the MetaBrain cortex meta-analysis (effective n up to approximately 2,700) (GTEx Consortium, 2020; Kerimov et al., 2021; de Klein et al., 2023). Colocalization was assessed with coloc.abf under default priors, and a locus was considered colocalized with a dataset if any gene reached PP4 > 0.8 (Methods).

Twenty-seven loci (9.6%; 95% CI 6.7-13.6%) colocalized with an eQTL in GTEx cortex, and 55 (19.6%; 15.4-24.6%) in MetaBrain cortex (Figure 1b). Allowing any of the 13 GTEx brain tissues raised the proportion to 23.8%, and allowing any of the 14 bulk datasets to 32.0% (90/281; 26.8-37.7%). Established positive controls behaved as expected; the strongest single colocalization in the study was FURIN at rs4702 (PP4 = 0.99996 in MetaBrain), a locus with prior experimental validation (Figure 1d). The two cortex resources agreed only partially at the locus level: 14 loci colocalized in both GTEx cortex and MetaBrain, 41 in MetaBrain only, and 13 in GTEx only (Figure 1c).

The failures were not attributable to an absence of local regulatory variation. Of the 191 loci without colocalization in any bulk dataset, 180 (94%) contained at least one gene with a brain eQTL at p < 10^-6^ within the locus window. At these loci the posterior mass concentrated on the hypothesis of two distinct causal variants (PP3) rather than on the absence of an eQTL signal.

### Single-variant colocalization misclassifies loci in both directions

coloc.abf assumes at most one causal variant per trait per region. To assess the consequences of this assumption we fine-mapped each locus with SuSiE, using LD from 503 European-ancestry 1000 Genomes samples, and paired individual GWAS signals with the eQTL Catalogue's published per-gene SuSiE results using coloc.bf_bf (Wang et al., 2020; Zou et al., 2022; Wallace, 2021). Convergence was reached at 279 of 281 loci. Multiple independent credible sets were common: 113 loci (40%) carried two or more, and up to five were observed (Figure 2a).

Allelic heterogeneity did not, however, preferentially explain the colocalization failures. The proportion of multi-signal loci was similar among non-colocalizing (40.8%) and colocalizing (38.9%) loci (Fisher p = 0.80). The principal effect of signal-level analysis was reclassification in both directions. Among the 85 loci that coloc.abf called colocalized within the six datasets with published eQTL fine-mapping, 23 (27%) were not supported when signals were paired individually; at these loci the fine-mapped GWAS and eQTL signals were distinct, as illustrated by ENSG00000232710 at rs9607782, where an aggregate PP4 of 0.97 resolved into two signals approximately 30 kb apart with PP3 = 0.98 (Figure 2c). Conversely, 23 loci that failed single-variant colocalization were recovered when their component signals were tested separately, for example rs4129585 (PP4 0.56 to 0.99). Roughly a quarter of colocalization classifications therefore depend on the causal-variant assumption, although the aggregate rate is little changed.

### eQTL sample size increases colocalization with diminishing returns

To isolate the effect of eQTL power we compared colocalization rates across five cortex datasets spanning a sample-size gradient: GTEx DLPFC (n = 175; 5.7% of loci), BrainSeq (479; 11.4%), ROSMAP (560; 17.1%), CommonMind (586; 16.4%), and MetaBrain (approximately 2,700; 19.6%) (Figure 3a). In a logistic model, the odds of colocalization increased 2.5-fold per tenfold increase in sample size (p = 3.8 x 10^-6^). The relationship flattened at the upper end of the range: a 3.4-fold increase in n from 175 to 586 tripled the colocalization rate, whereas the further 4.6-fold increase to MetaBrain added three percentage points. Extrapolation of the fitted curve suggests that bulk cortex expression studies plateau near 25% of loci. Power is therefore a genuine but bounded component of the missing regulation.

### Cell-type and developmental context contribute few additional loci

Cell-type-specific eQTLs from eight brain cell types (Bryois et al., 2022; n = 192 donors) colocalized with 37 loci, dominated by excitatory neurons (23 loci) and oligodendrocytes (12). Thirteen of these loci were missed by both bulk cortex resources, and six were missed by all 14 bulk datasets. Relative to its sample size this yield is high (GTEx DLPFC detected 16 loci at a comparable n), which suggests that the cell-type channel will grow substantially as single-cell cohorts expand.

Fetal cortex eQTLs (Walker et al., 2019; n = 211) colocalized with 20 loci, of which five were among the bulk-expression failures. Annotation of GWAS credible sets with Roadmap chromatin states provided no support for the hypothesis that non-colocalizing loci are concentrated in fetal-specific regulatory elements. Credible sets at non-colocalizing loci showed lower overlap with fetal regulatory chromatin than those at colocalizing loci (PIP-weighted fraction 0.084 versus 0.138; Mann-Whitney p = 0.0011), and the depletion was stronger still for adult regulatory chromatin (0.048 versus 0.136; p < 10^-4^), with no difference in fetal-not-adult overlap (p = 0.89) (Figure 3c). Consistently, credible-set variants at non-colocalizing loci were depleted of Bonferroni-significant fetal brain mQTLs (OR 0.30, p = 6 x 10^-4^) (Hannon et al., 2016). The credible sets of the unexplained loci are thus not hiding in fetal enhancers; they reside disproportionately in sequence not annotated as regulatory in any surveyed brain context.

### DNA methylation colocalizes at most loci that lack expression colocalization

Splicing QTLs from 17 brain datasets colocalized with 47 loci, including 15 of the 191 bulk-expression failures; the largest contributions again came from the best-powered datasets (ROSMAP, 22 loci; CommonMind, 20).

The largest single contribution came from DNA methylation. Using the Brain-mMeta meQTL meta-analysis (Qi et al., 2018; n of approximately 1,160 brains), 193 of 281 loci (69%) colocalized with at least one CpG, including 118 of the 191 loci (62%) without bulk expression colocalization (Figure 4). This excess is not an artifact of the number of methylation traits tested per locus. The number of tests per locus was comparable between the two data types (median 246 CpGs, approximately 389 meQTL tests, versus approximately 560 gene-by-dataset expression tests), whereas the per-test colocalization rate was six times higher for methylation (1.22% versus 0.21%). The result was also robust to threshold: at PP4 > 0.9, 157 loci retained methylation colocalization (90 among the expression failures), and at PP4 > 0.95, 130 loci (68 among the failures).

Attributing each locus sequentially to the first channel that explained it (bulk expression, multi-signal rescue, single-cell, fetal, splicing, methylation) accounted for 216 of 281 loci: 90, 23, 6, 4, 9, and 84 loci respectively (Figure 4a). Sixty-five loci (23%) remained unexplained by every tested channel. We note that colocalization between a risk variant and a meQTL demonstrates a shared causal variant, not that methylation mediates the disease effect; the direction and mechanism of the association are not identified by this analysis.

### Methylation signals nominate candidate genes at loci without eQTL support

A locus explained only by methylation identifies an epigenetically active risk variant but not a target gene. To recover candidate genes we performed a transitive analysis: for each CpG that colocalized with the GWAS, we tested whether the same meQTL signal also colocalized with each gene's expression in MetaBrain cortex, and we scored each gene by the smaller of the two posterior probabilities (GWAS-CpG and CpG-gene). This design exploits the fact that the meQTL, which is better powered than the GWAS, can support a colocalization with a modest eQTL that the GWAS itself cannot.

The chain analysis nominated at least one gene at 116 loci, with high-confidence support (both links PP4 >= 0.8) at 101 loci and a median of two genes per locus. Forty-four of the loci without any direct expression colocalization received high-confidence nominations; at these loci the best direct GWAS-eQTL posterior had a median of only 0.24. The nominations recovered biologically established candidates, including ZBED4 at rs6010045 (a locus at which the direct eQTL analysis had assigned PP3 = 0.76 despite an eQTL at p = 4 x 10^-10^; Figure 4c), ANKK1 adjacent to DRD2, HYKK in the CHRNA5 region, and LY6H at rs4129585. A minority of nominations lie far from their supporting CpG (in some cases more than 500 kb), and the full table therefore reports CpG-to-TSS distance and direct expression support for filtering (Table 2; Supplementary Table 5).

### Sensitivity analyses

Restricting the GWAS to the European-ancestry subset changed colocalization classifications at 3.6% of locus-dataset pairs for GTEx cortex and 6.1% for MetaBrain (Spearman correlation of posteriors 0.89 for both), indicating that the ancestry composition of the primary meta-analysis does not drive the results. Diagnostics for mismatch between the GWAS and the LD reference panel (kriging residuals) flagged a median of 2.0% of variants per locus, with no locus exceeding 5%, supporting the reliability of the fine-mapping-based analyses. All headline proportions were stable across PP4 thresholds of 0.5 to 0.9 (Supplementary Table 4).

## Discussion

Applied to the largest available schizophrenia GWAS, colocalization with bulk brain eQTLs assigns a candidate gene to roughly one locus in three, in line with previous reports of the variant-to-gene gap. The analyses reported here were designed to apportion the remainder among competing explanations within one framework, and three conclusions emerge.

First, the standard explanations are real but insufficient. eQTL sample size has a strong and significant effect on colocalization, yet the fitted relationship saturates near a quarter of loci, well short of closing the gap. Allelic heterogeneity is pervasive, and accounting for it corrects a substantial fraction of individual classifications in both directions, but multi-signal architecture is no more common at failing loci than at colocalizing ones. Cell-type-specific, fetal, and splicing QTLs each contribute, but their combined sequential contribution in current datasets is 19 loci.

Second, the majority of loci that resist expression-based explanation nonetheless share causal variants with DNA methylation in adult brain. The magnitude of this effect was unexpected: methylation colocalized at 62% of the expression-orphan loci, at a per-test rate several times that of expression, and robustly across thresholds. Together with the observation that credible sets at these loci are depleted of annotated regulatory chromatin, the parsimonious interpretation is that most of the missing regulation is not missing at the level of chromatin. The risk variants act on the brain epigenome, and it is the transcriptional consequence, presumably restricted to cell states, developmental windows, or conditions not represented in current expression catalogs, or buffered at steady state, that the eQTL studies fail to observe. This interpretation is consistent with proposals that eQTL and GWAS variants occupy systematically different regulatory contexts (Connally et al., 2022) and extends them by showing that a specific, measurable epigenetic signature marks the discrepant loci.

Third, methylation provides a practical route to gene nomination at loci where eQTL evidence is absent. The transitive chain analysis recovered known effector genes and produced high-confidence candidates at 44 loci that lack direct expression support. These nominations rest on a shared-variant argument along two links rather than on demonstrated mediation, and they should be treated as prioritization rather than proof; nevertheless, they convert a third of the previously intractable loci into testable gene-level hypotheses.

Several limitations warrant emphasis. Colocalization with methylation, like all colocalization, is symmetric with respect to mechanism, and reverse causation or horizontal pleiotropy at the chromatin level cannot be excluded. The meQTL analysis inherits the single-causal-variant assumption because fine-mapped meQTL summary data are not publicly available; by analogy with the expression results, a minority of the methylation colocalizations would likely be revised by signal-level analysis. The reference LD panel (n = 503) limits the sensitivity of GWAS fine-mapping, and 33 loci yielded no credible set. The MetaBrain and Bryois analyses required documented approximations (Z-derived effect sizes with borrowed allele frequencies, and p-value-based colocalization with a fixed sample size, respectively). The sequential attribution order is a presentational choice; because the channels overlap, alternative orders redistribute the intermediate counts, although the size of the unexplained residue is order-invariant. Finally, this is a single-trait analysis by a single analyst; the framework is fully automated and public, and its application to other traits would test the generality of the methylation finding.

The 65 unexplained loci merit attention in their own right. They are depleted of annotated regulatory chromatin in every brain context surveyed here, which suggests regulation in unassayed contexts, long-range or trans effects, or mechanisms outside the cis-regulatory model altogether. Molecular assays in stimulated, developmental, or rare cell populations, and QTL maps for additional chromatin marks, are the natural next experiments for this set.

## Methods

**Loci and GWAS summary statistics.** Genome-wide significant loci (n = 287) and their boundaries were taken from Supplementary Table 3 of Trubetskoy et al. (2022), verified against a pinned checksum. The extended MHC region (chr6:25-34 Mb, one locus) and five chromosome X loci were excluded, leaving 281. Primary (EUR+EAS) autosomal summary statistics (7,585,077 variants) were downloaded from the PGC; 7,585,069 variants passed quality filters (finite effect sizes, valid frequencies and p-values). Coordinates were lifted from GRCh37 to GRCh38 with CrossMap (99.94% of variants mapped; variants mapping to alternative contigs or different chromosomes were removed). Index-variant positions were verified by requiring agreement between the lifted coordinate and the position of the same rsID in the lifted file (281/281). Analysis windows were defined as the index variant plus or minus 1 Mb.

**Expression, splicing, and single-cell QTL data.** Nominal cis-QTL summary statistics for 13 GTEx v8 brain tissues, BrainSeq, CommonMind, ROSMAP, and fetal neocortex (Walker et al., 2019), and leafcutter splicing QTLs for 17 brain datasets, were obtained from the eQTL Catalogue by remote tabix query of the locus windows. MetaBrain cortex-EUR summary statistics (2020-05-26 release) were downloaded in full and windowed locally; meta-analysis effect sizes and standard errors were used directly, with minor allele frequencies taken from PGC3 control frequencies at matched variants because the public release contains no frequencies. Cell-type eQTLs for eight brain cell types (Bryois et al., 2022) were downloaded from Zenodo and joined to positions, alleles, and frequencies via the accompanying variant table.

**Colocalization.** Pairwise colocalization used coloc.abf (coloc 5.2.3) with default priors (p1 = p2 = 10^-4^, p12 = 10^-5^). The GWAS was modeled as a case-control trait with logistic effect sizes and their standard errors; QTL datasets were modeled as quantitative traits with effect sizes and standard errors where available and with p-values, frequencies, and sample sizes otherwise. Genes with fewer than 25 variants shared with the GWAS after allele-aware matching on GRCh38 position were not tested and are reported as such. A locus was scored against a dataset by the maximum PP4 across genes.

**Fine-mapping and signal-level colocalization.** GWAS fine-mapping used susie_rss (susieR 0.14.2; L = 10, coverage 0.95, estimate_residual_variance = FALSE) with LD computed per locus from 503 unrelated European-ancestry 1000 Genomes high-coverage samples (biallelic SNPs, MAF >= 0.005), with per-variant allele alignment between the GWAS and the panel. LD-mismatch diagnostics used kriging_rss. Signal-level colocalization paired GWAS credible-set components with the eQTL Catalogue's published per-gene SuSiE log Bayes factors via coloc.bf_bf, taking the best PP4 over signal pairs.

**Methylation QTLs.** The Brain-mMeta meQTL meta-analysis (Qi et al., 2018) was downloaded in SMR BESD format and windowed with the SMR --query interface (GRCh37 windows around the lifted index positions). CpG-level colocalization used effect sizes, standard errors, and allele frequencies from the file with n = 1,160, joined to the GWAS by rsID; because coloc.abf joins variants on identifier and position, GWAS coordinates were used for both datasets. Fetal brain mQTL overlap used the Bonferroni-significant associations of Hannon et al. (2016), which do not permit colocalization, and was analyzed as credible-set overlap.

**Chromatin annotation.** GWAS credible-set variants were annotated with Roadmap 15-state chromHMM segmentations (hg38 liftover) for fetal brain (E081, E082) and adult dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (E073). Regulatory states were TssA, TssAFlnk, EnhG, and Enh. Per-locus scores were PIP-weighted fractions of credible-set mass in regulatory states.

**Transitive gene nomination.** For each CpG with GWAS colocalization at PP4 > 0.8, colocalization was tested between the meQTL and each MetaBrain gene in the window (variants joined by rsID; meQTL coordinates used for both sides). The chain score for a locus-gene pair was min(GWAS-CpG PP4, CpG-gene PP4), maximized over CpGs; scores >= 0.8 were labeled high confidence and >= 0.5 suggestive.

**Sequential attribution.** Loci were attributed to channels in a fixed order chosen before inspection of the results (bulk expression; multi-signal rescue; single-cell; fetal; splicing; methylation), each locus counting once at the first channel with PP4 > 0.8 evidence.

**Sensitivity analyses.** The European-ancestry GWAS subset was processed identically and re-tested against GTEx cortex and MetaBrain. Threshold sensitivity was assessed at PP4 cutoffs of 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, and 0.9.

**Pipeline.** All analyses are orchestrated by Snakemake from configuration files, with pinned conda environments; every figure and table regenerates from raw public downloads. Data provenance (URLs, sizes, checksums) is recorded at download time.
